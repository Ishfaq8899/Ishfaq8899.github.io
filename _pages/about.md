Thank you for visiting my website!

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar at the [Knight Cancer Institute](https://www.ohsu.edu/knight-cancer-institute), [Oregon Health & Science University (OHSU)](https://www.ohsu.edu/), working with Jane Lange and Shelley Tworoger. My research uses simulation-based modeling to quantify the population-level benefits and harms of cancer screening, including early detection, overdiagnosis, and risk stratification, to inform screening policy.

At OHSU, I developed **[MCEDsim](https://github.com/Ishfaq8899/MCEDsim)**, an R package for simulating the population impact of multi-cancer early detection (MCED) testing, with natural history models calibrated to SEER incidence data across 15 solid cancer sites. I am currently building a lung cancer microsimulation model to understand how screening uptake, adherence, and treatment access contribute to racial/ethnic disparities in outcomes.

Before joining OHSU, I was a Postdoctoral Associate at the Yale School of Public Health, where I developed a microsimulation model of MGUS-to-multiple myeloma progression using SEER-Medicare data. I received my Ph.D. in Information Science (Mathematical Epidemiology) from Tohoku University, Japan, where I studied how isolation capacity shapes epidemic dynamics.

Research interests
------
* **Medical decision making & screening policy**: natural history models for cancer screening evaluation
* **Mathematical epidemiology**: mechanistic models of disease transmission and intervention
* **Integrated modeling for population health**: bringing infectious disease and cancer modeling together to inform guidelines

Methods
------
Microsimulation, continuous-time Markov chains, model calibration and validation, survival modeling, and R package development, using data sources such as SEER, SEER-Medicare, NLST, and PLCO.
